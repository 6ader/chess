## Chess Pieces and Their Basic Movements


<ul>
  <li> A pawn moves straight, one square at a time. But when it captures, it moves in a slant.</li>
  <li> A rook moves straight across the board, either forward, backward, or sideways.</li>
  <li> A bishop moves only in slanting lines and stays on the same color.</li>
  <li> A knight moves in an L shape and can jump over other pieces.</li>
  <li>A  queen can move straight or diagonally, which makes her very powerful.</li>
  <li>A king moves just one square in any direction, but protecting him is the whole game.</li>
</ul>


## Capturing Pieces
<ul>
    <li>When your piece moves onto a square with an opponent’s piece, that piece is gone.</li>
    <li>Most pieces capture the same way they move.</li>
    <li>Pawns are different. They move straight but capture diagonally.</li>
</ul>


## How the Game Ends
<ul>
    <li>The main goal is to trap the king.</li>
    <li>Checkmate: The king is attacked and has no escape. Then the game is over.</li>
    <li>Resign: A player can quit if the position is lost.</li>
    <li>Draw: Sometimes no one wins. This happens in cases like a stalemate.</li>
<ul>
